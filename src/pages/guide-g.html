Crypto Security Testing and Forensics in the UAE

Offensive security testing prevents; forensics investigates. Testing finds weaknesses in an exchange, wallet infrastructure, key management or smart contract before an attacker uses them. Forensics traces what happened after a theft: it follows funds on the blockchain, preserves evidence and produces a report. UAE virtual asset firms often need both, from the same or different providers.

Testing versus forensics

Testing versus forensics
Offensive testingForensics
Question answeredCan an attacker break in, and how?What happened, who moved the funds and where did they go?

Neither guarantees an outcome. Testing reduces exposure but does not prove absence of flaws. Tracing can follow funds but does not guarantee their recovery.

What gets tested in a crypto firm

A virtual asset firm has more attack surface than a typical web business. A thorough scope covers these areas.

  • Exchange or trading platform. Authentication, session handling, order logic, withdrawal flows, rate limiting and abuse of promotions.
  • APIs. Authorisation on every endpoint, key handling, parameter tampering, and any API used by partners or market makers.
  • Wallet infrastructure. Hot and cold wallet architecture, withdrawal approval workflows, signing services and the network paths to them.
  • Key management. Storage, access control, rotation and recovery of private keys, including hardware security modules or multi-party schemes where used.
  • Back-office and admin tooling. Panels used to approve withdrawals, adjust balances or manage listings. Access to them is often the shortest path to funds, so test their authentication, role separation and audit logging.
  • Mobile apps. Local storage of secrets, certificate pinning, tampering and reverse engineering of the app.
  • Operator and developer devices. Laptops and phones of staff who can deploy code, approve withdrawals or reach signing systems. A social engineering or assumed-breach test shows whether one compromised device reaches funds.
  • Smart contracts. A smart contract audit reviews code and logic, usually before deployment. A penetration test of the surrounding system, such as the front end, the oracle feeds, the admin keys and the integrations, is a different engagement. Many firms need both.

Name each of these in the scope, or the provider will test only what the quote lists.

Typical attack paths against virtual asset firms are well known in outline: phishing of operators with access to signing systems, theft or misuse of API keys, flaws in withdrawal approval logic, compromised developer or signing devices, and weaknesses in third-party integrations. A scope that follows these paths tests the places where value can actually leave the firm.

Forensics in practice

  1. Preserve. Secure logs, device images, wallet addresses and transaction hashes. Record who handled what and when, so that evidence stays usable.
  2. Analyse the wallet. Reconstruct how the attacker gained access: leaked key, compromised device, phishing or a flaw in the platform.
  3. Trace the funds. Follow transactions on the blockchain across addresses, mixers and exchanges, and identify points where funds touched a regulated service.
  4. Report. Document the method, findings and confidence level in a form a technical reader can verify.
  5. Support action. Provide the report to your management, your counsel or an authority, and answer questions on method. An expert report states what was established and what was not.

A forensics report usually contains the incident timeline, the addresses and transaction hashes involved, the method used to attribute and trace movements, the confidence level of each conclusion, the evidence list and any limits on the analysis. A reader should be able to follow each step and reproduce it.

Evidence handling matters as much as analysis, because a report built on altered or undocumented evidence is hard to use. Chain of custody is the record that links each item of evidence to the person who collected it, the time, the method and the place it was stored. In a wallet investigation it covers exported logs, device images, exchange account records and the queries run against blockchain data. A hash value recorded for each file at collection lets a later reader confirm that nothing changed.

UAE context

This section summarises primary pages and is not legal advice.

  • VARA. The Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority is the Dubai regulator for virtual assets. Its site says it is the sole authority for virtual assets across Dubai's free zones and mainland, except within the DIFC, and that it publishes a public register of licensed virtual asset service providers. The pages reviewed do not set out technical security controls. Read the VARA rulebook for your licence category.
  • DIFC and other regulators. Virtual asset activity inside the DIFC falls outside VARA's remit as stated on its site. Other virtual asset or payment-token activity may fall under other federal or free zone authorities. Confirm which authority regulates your activity.
  • Personal data. Customer data held by an exchange is personal data. The UAE Government portal describes the PDPL as applying to processing of personal data and setting general security duties (UAE Government portal).
  • Testing and licence. None of the primary pages reviewed names penetration testing as a licence condition. Whether your licence conditions or insurers require it is a question for your regulator and counsel.

See the requirement map in Guide C for the other UAE rules.

Questions to ask a provider

  • Do you test exchange platforms, APIs and wallet infrastructure, and who performs that work?
  • Do you cover key management and signing workflows, or only the web layer?
  • Do you review smart contracts, or only the surrounding system? Is the engagement an audit or a penetration test?
  • Is forensics a stated service? What does the deliverable look like: tracing report, evidence record, expert report?
  • How do you handle evidence: chain of custody, storage, access controls?
  • Which blockchains do you trace, and how do you state the confidence level of an attribution?
  • What will you not cover, and what rules of engagement apply to production systems?
