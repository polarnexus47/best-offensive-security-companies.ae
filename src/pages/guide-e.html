Offensive Security Certifications and Accreditations Explained

Credentials in offensive security work at three levels. Company-level accreditations, such as CREST, assess an organisation. Individual certifications, such as OSCP or CREST CRT, assess a person. Scheme-level programmes, such as the Dubai Cyber Force, register accredited providers for a defined buyer group. Each proves something narrow. None proves red team experience on its own, so verify the credential and ask about the people.

Reading the table: a credential answers one narrow question. CREST accreditation answers "has this organisation been assessed?". OSCP answers "did this person complete this practical exam?". PCI QSA answers "can this company assess PCI DSS compliance?". Matching each credential to the question it answers keeps a long list of logos from standing in for evidence of capability.

How to verify each credential

A logo on a website is a claim. The issuing directory is the proof.

  • CREST accreditation. Search the CREST Marketplace, where all members are listed, and read the services and accreditations shown for the company. CREST describes the Marketplace as the place to find accredited providers.
  • Dubai Cyber Force. CREST's Dubai page links to the list of registered companies. For the current mandatory status and scope, ask DESC.
  • PCI QSA. Check the PCI Security Standards Council's list of assessor companies.
  • Individual certifications. Ask for the person's name, certificate and verification link. The issuing body, such as OffSec, CREST or EC-Council, runs the check.
  • ISO/IEC 27001. Ask for the certificate, then check the scope statement and the certification body.

Treat "stated by the company" and "listed in a directory" as two different levels of evidence. The ranking labels them differently for that reason.

Questions to put in a tender about credentials: Which accreditations does the company hold, and in which directory can we check them? Which certifications do the named testers hold, and can we see the certificates? Do the people holding the certifications work on our project? A team list with many certifications is only useful if those people are the ones assigned.

Credentials versus experience

A certification shows that someone passed an examination on a given date. Red team work is different: it depends on judgement under uncertainty, on tradecraft, and on reporting that a defender can act on. An exam cannot measure all of that.

Ask instead:

  • Which engagements of this type has the assigned team run?
  • Can you show a sanitised report and a debrief outline?
  • Who will lead my project, and what have they delivered before?
  • What research or technical write-ups has the team published?

A company can also hold no accreditation and still deliver strong work, and a company with several can deliver a narrow test. That is why the ranking on this site does not use accreditations to set the order.

The Dubai Cyber Force page itself separates the two. It lists organisation-level requirements, such as a UAE trade licence including cyber security activities and CREST accreditation, apart from team member certifications such as CREST CRT, OSCP, ECSA, LPT, GPEN and GWAPT, and team leader certifications such as CREST CCT, OSEP, OSWE and GXPN.

How the ranking treats accreditations

The ranking of offensive security companies in the UAE shows accreditations as reference information. They do not decide the order. A company without a stated accreditation can be a strong specialist, and a company with several can have a narrow testing line. The methodology explains the ordering rule.
