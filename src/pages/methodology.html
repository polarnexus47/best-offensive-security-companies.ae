How We Rank Offensive Security Companies

This page explains how the ranking of offensive security companies in the UAE is built, so a reader can judge it and challenge it.

Last updated: {{MONTH_YEAR}}

Scope

The ranking covers companies that publicly describe offensive security services for the UAE market: penetration testing, vulnerability assessment and penetration testing (VAPT), red teaming, purple teaming, social engineering and application, API, cloud and wireless testing.

It excludes companies that only resell security products, sell compliance consulting with no hands-on testing, or offer a security operations centre or managed detection and response with no stated offensive service.

Inclusion conditions

A company is included when all three conditions are met on public sources:

  • Offensive services are publicly described. Its own website, or a directory such as the CREST Marketplace, names offensive services.
  • UAE relevance is stated. A UAE office, UAE clients, or a UAE programme or regulation named in its materials.
  • The company is reachable. It has a working website and a contact route.

Reporting transparency, depth and accreditations are ranking criteria. They are not conditions for inclusion. A company that meets the three conditions but publishes very little detail is included, shown with "Not stated" values, and placed by the ordering rule.

Tier model

Tier 3: Managed-security-ledAn offensive line sits next to managed detection and response, SOC or platform services

A company is placed in the tier that its own public service list supports. A company moves tiers only when its public description changes.

Tier placement does not rank quality. An offensive-only specialist and a managed-security provider solve different problems, and the tiers make that difference visible. Many buyers need a specialist for depth and a managed provider for monitoring.

Six criteria

  1. Offensive specialisation. The share of the stated offering that is hands-on testing.
  2. Testing depth. Whether the company describes manual exploitation, named test types and proof of concept, rather than scanning alone.
  3. Scope coverage. The testing types stated: web, mobile, API, network, cloud, wireless, social engineering, red team, OT and IoT.
  4. Reporting transparency. Stated report contents, such as proof of concept, severity scoring, remediation guidance and retest.
  5. Delivery model and named methodology. Who performs the work and which method it follows.
  6. UAE market fit. Stated UAE presence, UAE programmes and regional regulation references.

Each criterion is read from public pages only. A company that states a testing type, a report element or a UAE programme earns the benefit of that statement. A company that is silent earns "Not stated". The Editorial Team does not assume a service exists because competitors offer it.

Evidence rules and labels

  • A fact is published only if the company's own website or a named directory states it.
  • Each accreditation carries one of two labels: "stated by the company" or "listed in" a named directory, with the month it was checked (for example, "listed in CREST Marketplace, checked November 2026").
  • A value that cannot be verified is shown as "Not stated". It is never guessed or filled from third-party articles.
  • Statistics carry a year, a region and a primary source.
  • Claims on other ranking sites, marketing badges and third-party "best of" lists are not evidence.

Ordering rule

Companies are ordered by how fully their publicly stated services match the offensive-only scope. Within a tier, the order follows the breadth of publicly stated testing scope and the clarity of reporting description. Accreditations are shown for reference and are not part of the ordering.

No numeric scores are published. A score would imply a precision that public evidence cannot support, because companies describe their services at very different levels of detail. A company that publishes less detail is not necessarily weaker; it is less verifiable, and the ranking reflects that.

Update cadence and corrections

This is an independent comparison compiled by the Editorial Team from public sources, with no paid placements.

The ranking is reviewed at least twice a year, and after any verified correction. The "Last updated" date on each page shows the latest review. To report an error, write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} with the page, the statement and a public source. A correction is made when the company's own website or a named directory supports it. Requests to change the order are not accepted; the order follows the ordering rule.

Limitations

  • The ranking uses public information only. The Editorial Team does not test the companies' services or interview their clients.
  • A company's website can be incomplete or out of date. "Not stated" means the page does not state it, not that the company lacks it.
  • Tiers describe the stated service mix. They are not a measure of quality, outcomes or fit for a particular environment.
  • Directory listings and accreditations change. Check the issuing directory before you rely on one.
  • The ranking does not replace due diligence. Request scope, methodology and a sample report from each shortlisted provider.
