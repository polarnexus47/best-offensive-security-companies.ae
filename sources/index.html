How facts were checked

  1. Open the primary page on the day of review and read the statement in context.
  2. Record what the page says, not what other sites say about it.
  3. Label the evidence level: stated by the company, or listed in a directory with the check date.
  4. Mark gaps. A fact the page does not state is shown as "Not stated". A regulation page that does not mention penetration testing is marked "No explicit pentest clause found".
  5. Re-check time-sensitive facts: programme status, versions, statistics and directory listings.

Facts that need extra care

  • Dubai Cyber Force status. Two CREST pages differ. One, dated July 2024, says the official implementation date was 1 July 2024 and that Dubai government, semi-government and critical information infrastructure entities must use certified companies. The Dubai programme page still says the programme is expected to become mandatory from 31 July 2024. The ranking reports both and refers readers to DESC for the current rule.
  • Breach cost figures. IBM publishes a new Cost of a Data Breach Report each year, with different regional figures. The ranking uses the 2026 Middle East figure and names its year and study period.
  • Standards versions. NIST SP 800-115 dates from September 2008. OWASP WSTG v4.2 is listed as the latest version, with release 5.0 in development.
  • Accreditations. A logo on a company site is a claim. The ranking shows directory-listed accreditations separately from company statements.
